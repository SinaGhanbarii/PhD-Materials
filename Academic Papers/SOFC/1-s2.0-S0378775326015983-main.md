These challenges have driven extensive research towards the development of alternative anode materials with enhanced structural stability, catalytic activity, and fuel tolerance. 

Among the different alternatives investigated, perovskite-based oxides have emerged as strong candidates for next-generation SOFC anode materials. Perovskite materials generally possess highly flexible crystal structure with the general formula ABO3, where the larger A-site is occupied by rare-earth metals or alkaline earth metals, while the smaller B-site cation is commonly occupied by transition metals. The adaptable nature of the perovskite lattice enables extensive substitution at both A and B sites, providing precise control over electrical conductivity, catalytic activity, oxygen vacancy concentration, structural stability and thermal expansion behavior for fuel-flexible SOFC applications. Depending on their composition and structural ordering, perovskite anodes are broadly classified into four categories: Single perovskite anodes (ABO3), which offer simple structures and tunable redox properties. Double perovskite anodes (A2BB'O6− δ), known for their high electrical conductivity and resistance to carbon build up and sulfur poisoning. Layered perovskite anodes (AA'B2O5+δ), which feature ordered A-site structures with high oxygen mobility and excellent catalytic activity making them suitable for ammonia and hydrocarbon oxidation reaction. Composite and exsolution engineered perovskite systems, where combinations with metals or secondary oxides further enhance performance, durability, and fuel flexibility. Together, these structural variations highlight the adaptability of perovskite materials, as schematically illustrated in Figs. 2 and 3, which present the crystal structures and classification of perovskite-based anode materials employed in SOFC applications. 

Beyond their compositional versatility, major advantage of perovskite anodes lies in their mixed ionic and electronic conductivity, which enables electrochemical reaction to occur at sites beyond the conven– tional triple-phase boundary (TPB) regions observed in Ni YSZ systems. Furthermore, oxygen vacancies enhance oxide ion transport and promote catalytic fuel oxidation while simultaneously suppressing carbon deposition on the electrode surface. Recent developments in nanoexsolution engineering have further boosted the performance of perovskite anodes by enabling in-situ formation of well anchored metallic nanoparticles in reducing conditions, as shown in Fig. 4 These socketed nanoparticles function as highly active catalytic centers with excellent resistance to sulfur poisoning, agglomeration, and carbon deposition, thereby greatly enhancing long term electrochemical stability [7–9]. 

Several review articles have addressed perovskite based SOFC anodes and their electrochemical behavior, however each covers only a fraction of the design space necessary to fuel-flexible applications. Shu et al. reviewed advanced perovskite anodes with primarily emphasis on single perovskites structures and their basic electrochemical performance, without systematic analysis on fuel-specific perovskites-based systems [5]. YahyaZadeh surveyed fossil fuel-derived SOFCs and methane reforming catalysis, covering alcohol and hydrocarbon fuels but omitting direct carbon and ammonia fuel systems [10].The review by Porotnikova and Osinkin provided an in-depth analysis of heteroatom substitution in Sr2Fe1.5Mo0.5O6− δ, remained limited to SFMO composition rather than offering comparative evaluation among various perovskite families [11]. Skutina et al. investigated undoped Sr2MMoO6 double perovskite molybdates (M = Ni, Mg, Fe) with emphasis on their synthesis route and transport properties but did not explore performance across varied fuel environments or compare these with single, layered, or exsolution-engineered perovskites [12]. Lo Faro et al. explored lanthanum ferrite-based exsolved perovskites for fuel-flexible anodes applications, yet review was confined to specific material family and did not encompass layered structures, double perovskites, or direct carbon 

and ammonia fuels [13]. Hussain and Li presented a comprehensive of SOFC anode, cathode, and electrolyte materials. However, they did not provide dedicated evaluation of perovskite anode design strategies for various fuel type [14]. Xia et al. directed attention toward recent developments in efficient perovskite ceramic anodes, but did not systematically address long-term durability, comparisons of perovskite families across different fuel environments and commercialization barriers [15]. Zainon et al. discussed challenges of perovskite-based anodes for different fuels but focused mainly on classification of material without critical cross-family comparison of cost, scalability or degradation mechanisms [1]. 

None of these reviews present an integrated, fuel-by-fuel comparative evaluation that simultaneously considers single perovskites, double perovskites, layered perovskites, and exsolution-engineered systems across the entire spectrum of SOFC fuels (H2, hydrocarbons, syngas, ammonia, alcohol, and direct carbon). Furthermore, key factors such as electrical conductivity, sulfur tolerance, polarization resistance, carbon resistance electrolyte compatibility, long-term durability, and costeffectiveness have not been systematically analyzed across perovskite families under versatile fuel environment. This review aims to bridge this gap by organizing the existing literature according to fuel type rather than material classification, providing critical comparisons of performance and degradation mechanisms among various perovskite families for each fuel, identifying key unresolved challenges along with future perspectives for advancing highly efficient and fuel-flexible SOFC technologies that differentiate this work from the aforementioned reviews. 

# **2. Perovskite based anodes for fuel flexible SOFCs** 

# _2.1. Hydrogen-fueled SOFCs_ 

Hydrogen is the most widely studied and fundamental fuel for solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) due to its clean electrochemical oxidation pathway, lack of carbon formation, and rapid reaction kinetics at elevated temperatures. In hydrogen based SOFCs, the anode is responsible for direct electrochemical oxidation of H2, producing water and releasing electrons. Despite its simplicity compared to hydrocarbonbased fuels, hydrogen operation still requires anodes with fast charge transfer kinetics, high catalytic activity and long-term redox stability under reducing conditions. To address the sulfur sensitivity of Nicontaining anodes in solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs), a lithiumconductive core-shell Li0.33La0.56TiO3 (LLTO) architecture synthesized via a solution infiltration method, exhibited significantly enhanced anodic activity due to Ti-O perovskite framework. It demonstrated stable operation for over 70 h at 800<sup>◦</sup> C in 1000 ppm H2S/H2 fuel, whereas cells with nanoparticle-based anodes failed after only 5.5 h under similar conditions [16]. Du et al., in 2018 synthesized Sr2FeMo2/3Mg1/3O6− δ a novel double perovskite for SOFC anode, where Mg doping enhanced redox stability in both oxidizing and reducing environment and promoted antisite defects, which improved electrochemical activity. The material delivered peak power densities of 531 to 1316 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> from 750 to 900<sup>◦</sup> C in humidified H2 along with good thermal stability, conductivity, and resistance to sulfur and carbon, supported by computational modeling indicating enhanced oxygen vacancy formation and migration crucial for SOFC anode [17]. Similarly, SrMo1-x FexO 3-δ<sup>oxides (where x=</sup> 0.1, 0.2) and partially Ba-substituted Sr0.9Ba0.1Mo0.9Fe0.1O3-δ have emerged as efficient anode candidates for IT-SOFCs with LSGM electrolyte. Ba substitution increases the lattice parameters and enhances diffusion of O<sup>2−</sup> ions within the lattice. The material exhibited Pm3m crystal structure with oxygen vacancies, which are essential for its mixed electronic and ionic conductivity. They achieved peak power density of 500 mWcm<sup>−2</sup> at 850<sup>◦</sup> C in pure hydrogen (H2) as fuel along with good thermal stability and compatibility with electrolyte [18]. Similarly, Jong Jun Lee et al. have investigated La0.4Sr0.4Ti0.94 Ni0.06O3-δ (LSTN) with in-situ exsolved Ni NPs as

The cell achieved a maximum power density of 0.46 W cm<sup>2</sup> at 850<sup>◦</sup> C with excellent redox stability. DFT analysis confirmed better H2 adsorption and dissociation on exsolved Ni sites, highlighting exsolution as a promising method to improve electrocatalytic performance [19]. Ti substitution in Sr2Fe1.4TixMo0.6− xO6− δ (x = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3) double perovskites was employed to increase the stability of SOFC electrode cermet by substituting Mo with Ti. The compounds maintained a stable crystal structure (Pm-3m space group) under both reducing and oxidizing conditions and exhibited high oxygen exchange capacity in air, moderate electrical conductivity and low thermal expansion coefficient, making it promising anode material in hydrogen-fueled energy conversion devices [20]. Further extending B-substitution strategy to improve transport and stability of perovskites anodes, Al incorporation was introduced to SrMoO3 to simultaneously enhance oxygen ion transport and catalytic oxidation of hydrogen. The SrMo0.8Al0.2O3-δ (SMAO-20) anode delivered a peak power density of 633 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> at 850<sup>◦</sup> C with LSGM electrolyte, with good chemical compatibility and redox reversibility. However, extended fuel stability tests were limited, and performance evaluation was mainly evaluated in pure H2, requiring further validation under practical fuels [21]. 

Cr-doped LaNiO3 compositions further highlight the role of cation substitution in improving electrochemical properties, electronic conductivity and structural stability for IT-SOFC applications. Compositions were synthesized using a modified Sol-gel Pechini route followed be sintering at different temperatures showed that higher Cr content increased porosity and structural stability [22]. Yang et al., in 2024 identified La0.75Sr0.25Co0.5Mn0.5O3-δ (LSCM) as a potential anode material due to its excellent ionic mobility and robust structural integrity. Synthesized via sol-gel method, it exhibited a stable orthorhombic Pnma structure, while Co substitution reduced band gap from 1.426 eV (oxidized state) to 1.177 eV (reduced state), thereby facilitated electronic conductivity. LSCM also showed enhanced electronic conductivity 5.25 Scm<sup>−1</sup> at 800<sup>◦</sup> C and delivered a peak power density of 0.36 W cm<sup>−2</sup> in H2 fuel, with DFT results confirmed improved charge transport [23]. To further enhance anode performance, Ru–SrMo0.9O3− δ containing exsolved Ru nanoparticles has been developed by Cascos et al., as excellent performance IT-SOFC anode. Oxygen deficient cubic structure provides mixed ionic and electronic conductivity, while segregated Ru NPs promote efficient hydrogen oxidation resulting electronic conductivity of 280 S cm<sup>−1</sup> leading to low polarization resistance and high-power density 840 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> . It also showed good thermal expansion compatibility with common electrolytes like LSGM, redox reversibility, and stable porous microstructure after operation, supporting good short-term durability. However, its practical deployment was limited by the high cost of Ru, limited information about sulfur and carbon tolerance, and insufficient long term degradation data under realistic fuel operating and cycling conditions [24]. Mn-doped SrMo1-xMnxO3-δ perovskites developed as promising anode materials for H2-fueled SOFCs, owing to their favorable transport and structural properties. The reduced perovskite materials exhibited a stable cubic perovskite structure, good thermal stability and electrical conductivity. Single cell testing with H2 fuel confirmed their potential for SOFC applications, highlighting the role of Mn doping in improving anode performance [25]. 

In perovskite-based SOFC anodes, hydrogen oxidation occurs through H2 adsorption and dissociation, followed by charge transfer through mixed ionic and electronic pathways and subsequent water formation at the anode surface. The reaction kinetics are enhanced by oxygen vacancies that facilitate ion transport, exsolved metallic nanoparticles that provide highly active and stable catalytic sites, extending the reaction site beyond the conventional TPB. Therefore, the development of ideal SOFC anodes requires fast hydrogen oxidation kinetics, an optimized oxygen vacancy concentration, high electronic conductivity, structural stability under reducing conditions, and strong tolerance to 

sulfur poisoning. Recent progress in perovskite, double perovskite, and exsolution-engineered anodes have resulted in improved power densities and enhanced operational durability in humidified H2 fuel. Collectively, these findings highlight that strategic B-site doping and exsolution engineering are effective approaches for developing robust, cost-effective, and fuel-flexible anodes capable of matching or surpassing the performance of conventional Ni-based SOFC systems. However, a critical gap still remains:although power densities have progressively improved from 0.36 W cm<sup>−2</sup> (LSCM) to 0.84 W cm-<sup>2</sup> (Ru-exsolved SrMoO3), the compositions with excellent chemical stability like LSCM and Sr2FeMoO6 continue to be constrained by moderate electronic conductivity, while the materials with highest-conductivity (Sr2Fe1.5Mo0.5O 6- δ) have seldom been tested in full or symmetric cell configurations under pure H2. Furthermore, long-term durability data beyond 100 h have been reported for only two compositions (LLTO core-shell at 70 h and LSTN at 100 h), highlighting the long-term stability of most hydrogen-specific perovskite anodes is still not adequately verified. No systematic investigation has compared the oxidation kinetics of hydrogen across single, double, and exsolution-engineered perovskites under identical cell configurations, making direct performance comparisons challenging. 

# _2.2. Hydrocarbon fueled SOFCs (CH4, C2H6 and C3H8)_ 

Hydrocarbon fuels including methane (CH4), ethane (C2H6), and propane (C3H8) are attractive for SOFC operation due to their wide availability, high energy density, and compatibility with existing fuel infrastructure. However, their direct electrochemical application was hindered by severe challenges including incomplete fuel oxidation, carbon deposition (coking), sulfur contamination, and catalyst degradation. These challenges are strongly influenced by reforming kinetics and fuel cracking reactions at the anode surface. Unlike hydrogen-fueled SOFCs, hydrocarbon systems require in situ reforming or catalytic oxidation pathways, where hydrocarbons are converted into intermediate species (H2 and CO) before electrochemical oxidation. Therefore, advanced anode materials must simultaneously provide high oxygen mobility, catalytic reforming activity, and resistance to carbon deposition. 

(PrBa)0.95(Fe0.9Mo0.1)2O 5+δ (PBFM), a cation-deficient, Ni-free layered perovskite, served as a robust electrode platform for direct hydrocarbon SOFCs. The synergistic interaction of A-site deficiency and Mo substitution at the B-site increases structural stability over diverse fuel environments and operating temperatures, while increasing oxygen-vacancy formation, promoting efficient mixed ionic and electronic conduction. The material exhibited exceptional resistance to carbon deposition and sulfur deactivation in organic fuels, maintaining high electrical conductivity. It achieves peak power densities of 0.54 W cm<sup>−2</sup> and 1.72 W cm<sup>−2</sup> using CH4 and H2 fuels, respectively [26]. Advancing this progress towards intermediate-temperature operation (550–700<sup>◦</sup> C), the Ni-free double perovskite Sr2FeNb0.2Mo0.8O6-δ (SFNM20) demonstrated complementary strengths, achieving peak power densities of 520 and 380 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> in H2 and CH4 at 800<sup>◦</sup> C, respectively. The cell maintains stable performance under various current loads in H2 and CH4 at 700<sup>◦</sup> C, alongside superior redox stability when exposed to fluctuating anode gas environment confirming its structural integrity across diverse fuel compositions as a viable candidate for high-efficiency SOFC electrodes [27]. Similarly, V. Cascos and colleagues in 2017 developed SrMo1-xGaxO3-δ (x = 0.1 and 0.2) as anode materials for IT-SOFCs. They achieve electrical conductivities of 268 S cm<sup>−1</sup> for x = 0.1 and 58 S cm<sup>−1</sup> for x = 0.2 at 850<sup>◦</sup> C. Single cells with these anodes produce power outputs near 900 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> at 850<sup>◦</sup> C using H2 and perform well with methane. Their thermal expansion, atmospheric reversibility, and electrolyte compatibility make them promising IT-SOFC anode [28]. 

Complementing these single component systems, the Ba0.3Sr0.7Fe0.9Mn0.1O3-δ (BSFM) perovskite anode exploited A-site and

In addition to this nanoalloy embedding approach, Faro et al. investigated exsolution from lanthanum ferrite-based perovskites, specifically La0.6Sr0.4Fe0.8Co0.2O3 doped with Ni. This resulted in a dual– phase structure consisting of Ruddlesden Popper phase alongside a solid-solution matrix (α-Fe100-ᵧ-zCoyNizOx) with robust electronic conductivity under reducing conditions. In realm of double perovskite systems, Sr2MgMoO6-δ (SMMO) has become a benchmark material due to its exceptional electronic conductivity, structural integrity, excellent electrocatalytic activity, structural integrity under reducing atmospheres, moderate coking resistance, and effective sulfur tolerance, making it one of the most widely studied fuel-flexible double perovskite anode [33]. To overcome the persistent challenge of thermal expansion mismatch, tungsten-doped (PrBa)0.95(Fe0.95W0.05)2O5+δ was engineered by substituting W at the B-site within layered double perovskite lattice framework. This modification reduced thermal expansion coefficient from 17.11 to 14.59 × 10<sup>−6</sup> K<sup>−1</sup> , significantly increasing compatibility with LSGM electrolyte. It further enhanced surface oxygen species, increased the oxygen surface exchange coefficient, and reduced polarization resistance to 0.123–0.199 Ω cm<sup>2</sup> at the anode and 0.03 –0.058 Ω cm<sup>2</sup> at the cathode at 800<sup>◦</sup> C. Material maintained stable operation maintained in humidified H2, syngas, ethane, and propane. The primary trade-off, however, was a decrease in electronic conductivity from 52.5 S cm<sup>−1</sup> to 32.5 S cm<sup>−1</sup> , reflecting that improved thermal compatibility in W-doped systems come at a measurable electronic cost 

[34]. Addressing the need for improved ionic conductivity n composite systems, CeO2 was introduced into La0.5Sr0.5Fe0.9Mo0.1O3-δ to form the LSFM-CeO2 composite anode. This strategy expanded theTPB, increased active site density, and improved overall conductivity, yielding peak power densities of 291 and 190 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> in H2 and ethane respectively with a BZCY electrolyte at 750<sup>◦</sup> C. Notably, the composite further achieved 93.4% ethylene selectivity and 37.1% ethane conversion yield with no carbon deposition over 22 h, underscoring its dual utility for simultaneous co-production of valuable chemicals and power generation [35]. 

Advancing the concept noble metal incorporation into perovskite, Zhai et al. developed a Ni-free PrBaFe1.9Pd0.1O5+ _δ_ (PBFPd0.1) layered perovskite anode where Pd doping at the B-site enhanced bulk electronic conductivity via electron delocalization and stronger Pd–O covalent bond and enabled in situ exsolution of uniformly dispersed Pd nanocatalysts (30 nm) under reducing conditions. DFT calculations − 0.14 eV on bare PBF to confirmed improved ethane adsorption energy ( − 0.77 eV on Pd@PBF), lowering activation resistance and boosting catalytic performance through interfacial Lewis acid/base interaction. Consequently, the optimized PBFPd0.1–SDC anode delivered peak power densities of 702 and 377 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> at 800<sup>◦</sup> C in H2 and near-dry ethane (3% H2O) respectively, outperforming both undoped and over-doped compositions due to improved resistance to coking and phase instability [36]. 

Overall, the reviewed hydrocarbon-fueled SOFC anodes demonstrate a clear shift from simple A-site substituted perovskites toward advanced exsolution-engineered hierarchical architectures, advancing peak power densities from below 0.7 W cm<sup>−2</sup> to 2.12 W cm<sup>−2</sup> along with operational stability extending beyond 200 h. These developments collectively confirm that noble metal exsolution, dual exsolution strategic B-site doping, and heterointerface engineering are the most effective levers for simultaneously achieving high catalytic activity, carbon tolerance, and long-term durability in hydrocarbon-fueled SOFC systems. However, the lack of consistent testing protocols among various studies (including variations in steam-to-carbon ratios, non-uniform cell geometries and different hydrocarbon partial pressures) makes it challenging to draw definitive comparison of carbon resistance among various perovskite families, a critical limitation that has not been addressed in previous review. 

# _2.3. Syngas fueled SOFCs_ 

Syngas is a mixture of CO and H2, generally derived from biomass, coal gasification, reforming, or hydrocarbon partial oxidation. It is an important intermediate fuel for SOFCs due to its high energy content and industrial availability. Unlike pure hydrogen systems, syngas-operated SOFCs require anode to support both hydrogen and carbon monoxide electrochemical oxidation, introducing additional challenges through carbon deposition via Boudouard reaction: 2CO → C + CO2, CO<sup>−</sup> induced poisoning of catalytic active zones, and complex multicomponent gas phase reforming equilibria. These challenges demand anode materials with strong CO oxidation capability, redox stability, mixed ionic and electronic conductivity and simultaneous resistance to sulfur contamination and carbon deposition under multi component fuel environments. 

Among double perovskite system developed to address these challenges, Sr1.9VMoO6–δ (SVMO) has emerged as a promising candidate due to its cubic symmetry and low thermal expansion coefficient 13.3 × 10<sup>−6</sup> K<sup>−1</sup> SVMO exhibits significantly higher electrical conductivity surpassing both conventional Ni/YSZ anode and other doubleperovskite materials. Pd nanoparticle impregnation Pd@SVMO proved particularly effective, demonstrated excellent redox stability alongside robust resistance to sulfur and carbon poisoning over 80-h test using H2S-containing syngas, highlighting external surface modification can substantially extend the durability of intrinsically promising double perovskite frameworks [37]Building on this foundation,

STFM0.5 demonstrates high electronic conductivity of 22.3 Scm<sup>−1</sup> in H2 at 800<sup>◦</sup> C, achieving maximum power densities of 547 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> and 215 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> with H2 and syngas respectively, with stable electrochemical performance without carbon deposition in syngas at 750<sup>◦</sup> C, and exhibited chemical and thermal compatibility with both LSGM and SDC electrolytes [38] Similarly, Sr2TiMoO6 (STMO) was engineered by deliberately integrating the complementary properties of SrMoO3 and SrTiO3 perovskites into a single double perovskite framework, combining the high electronic conductivity of Mo-based phases and structural stability of Ti-based systems. # _2.4. Alcohol fueled SOFCs_ 

Alcohols such as methanol (CH3OH), ethanol (C2H5OH), and higher bio alcohols are attractive SOFCs fuel owing to their liquid state, high hydrogen content, and potential for renewable production from biomass feedstocks. Compared to hydrocarbons, alcohol possess lower carbon to hydrogen ratio which makes them inherently less susceptible to severe carbon deposition; however, their electrochemical utilization in SOFCs remain non-trivial. Alcohol fuels undergo a sequence of complex anodeside-reactions involving dehydration, reforming, and partial oxidation producing intermediates including CO, H2 and oxygenated species, that must be efficiently oxidized before they accumulate and poison active sites. Consequently, ideal SOFC anodes for alcohol fuels must integrate fast oxygen mobility, resistance to intermediate poisoning, high reforming activity, resistance to intermediate poisoning, and long-term structural stability. Perovskite-based anodes materials are well positioned due to their mixed ionic and electronic conductivity (MIEC), oxygen vacancy engineering, and effective stabilization of catalytic surface phases. 

Among the single perovskite systems investigated, La0.75Sr0.25Cr0.5Mn0.5O3 (LSCM) based composite anodes established an important baseline, offering excellent chemical and redox stability, inherent carbon resistance, sulfur tolerance, and a thermal expansion coefficient compatible with ScSZ/YSZ electrolytes. However, their primary limitation was reduced electronic conductivity in reducing atmospheres leading to increase polarization and ohmic resistance was effectively addressed through impregnation of Cu and composite structuring with ionic conductors, which collectively improved catalytic activity for ethanol oxidation, increased TPB density and reduced polarization resistance to deliver stable power outputs of several100 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> . LSCM achieved 120-h coke-free operation in ethanol, surpassing durability typically achieved by Ni-based anodes under similar conditions establishing it as a cost-effective and structurally stable anode platform for alcohol-fueled SOFC, provided 

microstructural parameters such as porosity and particle size are carefully optimized [40]. Expanding on performance established by conventional perovskites, introduction of in situ co-exsolution in SrBiFeTiO6− δ (SBFT) system provided an additional route for enhancing electrochemical performance. Resulting anode displayed electrical conductivities of 24 and 26 S cm<sup>−1</sup> at 700 and 750<sup>◦</sup> C respectively in H2. Furthermore, the addition of SDC reduced polarization resistance from 2.15 Ω cm<sup>2</sup> to 0.09 Ω cm<sup>2</sup> . In situ exsolved nanoparticles further improved carbon resistance and catalytic activity, achieving peak power densities of 503 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> in H2 and 280 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> in ethanol with stable operation for over 30 h in ethanol, alongside good electrolyte compatibility, redox stability, and sulfur tolerance. However, residual polarization losses, moderate ethanol power output, and long-term risks associated with nanoparticle coarsening under sustained operation remain important limitations requiring further attention [41] Kimia Y. Javan et al. extended alcohol fuel compatibility towards intermediate operating temperatures (600-700<sup>◦</sup> C) and oxidation of methanol by developing Mo-doped La0.6Sr0.4Co0.2Fe0.8O3− δ (LSCFMo) as an alternative anode material. LSCFMo material shows improved coke resistance and phase stability. Electrochemical testing demonstrated that LSCFMo is an effective catalyst for methanol oxidation at 600<sup>◦</sup> C, achieving an OCV of 0.55 V using GDC as the electrolyte exhibiting zero carbon deposition. These findings suggest that LSCFMo is a promising anode material for renewable biofuel-fed SOFCs [42]. 

In nutshell alcohol-fueled SOFC anodes reviewed here are all single perovskite or simple composite systems (spanning LSCM-based composites, exsolution-enhanced SBFT, and Mo-doped LSCFMo), and their peak power densities in methanol and ethanol (0.139–0.394 W cm<sup>−2</sup> ) remain well below those achieved by double and layered perovskites for H2 and CH4 in the preceding sections. Nevertheless, long term coke free performance demonstrated by LSCM's without any exsolution strategy, highlights considerable untapped potential, Consequently, the systematic application of double perovskites, layered perovskites, and exsolution-engineered architectures to alcohol-specific oxidation represents a promising and largely unexplored research direction for advancing fuel-flexible SOFC technologies. 

# _2.5. Ammonia fueled SOFCs_ 

Ammonia (NH3) has gained significant attention as a promising carbon-free fuel for solid oxide fuel cells due to its high hydrogen content, ease of liquefaction, established global infrastructure, and potential for carbon-neutral energy conversion. Unlike hydrocarbon fuels, ammonia does not suffer from carbon deposition, making it inherently attractive for long-term SOFC operation. However, its electrochemical utilization is fundamentally governed by its catalytic decomposition into nitrogen and hydrogen, which subsequently participate in hydrogen oxidation reactions at the anode **.** Sr2CoMo0.8Ni0.2O6-δ (r-SCMN2) double perovskite is promising anode for direct ammonia SOFCs (DASOFCs). It exhibited high catalytic activity for ammonia decomposition and H2 oxidation, low polarization resistance, redox and anti-sintering stability, excellent carbon resistance, good compatibility with SDC electrolyte and outstanding durability. A peak power density of 350 mW cm<sup>−2</sup> at 800<sup>◦</sup> C in NH3 was achieved attributed to welldispersed NiCo nanoparticles providing abundant active sites. However, the ionic conductivity has not yet been explicitly analyzed. Sulfur tolerance and thermal cycling stability also remain untested. In addition, excessive Ni doping may lead to structural instability, and limited long-term testing remains a concern for practical deployment [9]. # **4. Challenges, commercialization barriers and future perspectives** 

Although great advances have been made in perovskite anodes for fuel-flexible SOFCs, there are still several scientific and technological challenges that need to be overcome for commercialization to be realized. While a lot of perovskite systems show good electrochemical activity, sulfur tolerance, carbon resistance and redox stability under laboratory scale operation, long-term operation under realistic industrial environment is not well explored. The challenges are structural degradation, thermal instability, nanoparticle coarsening, electrolyte compatibility and the complexity of the fabrication processes. 

One of the most important restrictions of SOFC's for commercial use is their long-term durability. Phase segregation, nanoparticle agglomeration, sulfur poisoning, thermal expansion mismatch, and microstructural instability are all degradation modes that cause perovskite anodes to deteriorate during prolonged operation. While exsolved nanoparticles have greater anchorage than is achieved with conventionally impregnated catalysts, repeated exposure at high temperature can lead to coarsening of the metallic nanoparticles, resulting in loss of catalytic activity and active surface area. Carbon deposition, sulfur contamination and oxygen vacancy blockage are especially problematic 

for SOFCs that are operated on hydrocarbon and syngas fuels. The presence of ash, sulfur containing intermediate and tar species also enhances degradation of the electrodes in direct carbon SOFCs. Redox cycling also causes severe mechanical stresses from volume changing within the electrode lattice leading to formation of cracks, delamination, and loss of conductivity in the electrode. Hence, future studies should focus on the composition of perovskites with high stability, strong interaction between metal and support, and high tolerance to thermal cycling and poisoning effects, which have a high concentration of oxygen vacancies. 

While some of the advanced perovskite anodes show promising laboratory results, their wide scale production and reproducibility are yet to be achieved. The preparation methods including nano-exsolution engineering, infiltration, dual-exsolution, atomic-level doping, and heterostructure assembly are quite complicated, require a high degree of control over the atmosphere, and require expensive preparation conditions. The uniform distribution, controlled porosity, phase purity and reproducibility of the particles in mass production remains a challenge. In addition, advanced perovskite anodes must be carefully matched to the SOFC stack materials in terms of their thermal expansion coefficients, mechanical strength, and chemical compatibility with SOFC electrolyte materials and interconnects to be integrated into practical SOFC stacks. However, for scale up production process, sintering methods for low cost, stable electrode structures, and simplified fabrication processes which could ensure high electrochemical performance under industrial operating conditions are required. 

One of the most significant challenges to the commercialization of perovskite-based SOFCs is the cost of the materials and availability of the resources. Several high-performance perovskite anodes are based on expensive transition metals like molybdenum, ruthenium, cobalt and palladium which make the fabrication cost high, in addition to their restricted large-scale implementation. Other rare-earth elements such as lanthanum, praseodymium and samarium also add to the economic difficulties. 

Noble-metal exsolution technologies are effective ways to enhance catalytic activity and sulfur tolerance, but the need for less reliance on the high-cost elements is crucial for practical commercialization. Alternative Earth abundant, economical materials with high electrochemical activity and stability and fuel flexibility should be examined in future studies. 

The recent progress in computational materials science opens new possibilities for discovering and optimizing SOFC anode materials. In perovskite systems, oxygen vacancy formation, electronic conductivity, adsorption energies, sulfur tolerance and fuel oxidation pathways have been investigated using density functional theory (DFT) calculations, which have proven useful. The reaction mechanism on the surface and catalytic activity can also be predicted by computational modeling prior to experimental synthesis, as can be done for thermodynamic stability. 

Furthermore, machine learning and AI aided optimization are emerging into effective strategies for high-throughput screening of perovskite compositions and accelerated materials discovery. # **5. Conclusion** 

SOFCs offer remarkable efficiency, fuel flexibility, yet their commercial deployment remains hindered by the limitations of conventional Nickel containing anodes, such as sulfur poisoning, carbon deposition, and poor redox stability. This review highlights the transformative role of perovskite-based anode materials, including single, double, and layered perovskite systems, as well as their composites and doped derivatives. These materials demonstrate superior structural stability, mixed ionic–electronic conductivity, and enhanced resistance against coking and sulfur poisoning, making them promising alternatives for next-generation SOFCs. However, challenges persist regarding longterm durability, scale-up synthesis, cost, and compatibility with diverse electrolytes. Future research should prioritize tailoring microstructures through exsolution strategies, multi-doping, and nanostructuring, while integrating computational modeling to guide material design. With these advancements, perovskite-based anodes hold the potential to unlock high-performance, fuel-flexible, and commercially viable SOFC technologies that can contribute significantly to clean and sustainable energy systems.
