[18] have developed a system that integrates anaerobic digestion technology with SOFCs (ADFC). The ADFC system can produce clean electricity with an output range of 480 kW to 1410 kW, providing an environmentally friendly and economical solution for solid waste treatment and energy production. In experimental studies, Cai et al. [19,20] have demonstrated a system that integrates SOFCs with pyrolysis-gasification processes to directly convert plastic/agricultural waste into electricity. The above works demonstrate the potential of SOFC-based systems for efficient energy recovery and high-value utilization of solid waste. 

However, few studies paid attention to the microscale transport and reaction processes within SOFCs fed by complex compositions of gasification gas, where many unknown problems may arise from electrochemical and chemical reactions, along with heat and mass transfer. A deeper understanding of these internal processes is essential for optimizing cell performance and stability. Owing to the limitations of experimental methods in analyzing these complex interactions, multiphysics coupling modeling offers an insightful vision for investigating the coupled effects of these processes and their overall impact on SOFC performance [21]. 

To fill the research gap outlined above, a multi-physics coupling model of SWGG-SOFCs has been developed in this study, as shown in Fig. 1. Through the established model, the electrochemical and chemical reactions, along with heat and mass transfer within SOFCs, are analyzed. The interaction between these processes and their combined impact on cell performance and stability are investigated under various operation 

conditions (different temperatures, voltages, and anodic inlet flow rates). Particular attention is paid to the internal temperature gradient, as it is closely associated with the stability of SOFCs. High temperature gradient may cause thermal stress and mechanical failure of SOFCs [22]. Two analytical approaches are applied: the One-factor-at-a-Time (OFAT) method and the Design of Experiments (DOE) method. The OFAT method varies a single parameter while keeping others constant, providing straightforward insights into individual factor effects but overlooking potential interactions [23,24]. In contrast, DOE accounts for parameter interactions, offering a more comprehensive understanding of system behavior [25]. Given the complex and highly nonlinear dependence of the temperature gradient on operating conditions, as revealed by the simulation results, DOE analysis is primarily applied to the thermal behavior rather than the electrochemical performance. Although the DOE approach has been widely adopted in SOFC studies [26,27], investigations specifically targeting temperature gradients and the interactions among influencing factors remain limited, particularly for SOFCs fueled with complex hydrocarbon-containing gases such as SWGG. The findings of this work therefore provide both theoretical insights and practical guidance for optimizing SWGG-SOFCs operation.

# _2.1.1. Mass transport, momentum transfer, heat transfer_ 

The governing equations and constitutive equations employed in this model are presented in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. Materials and geometric parameters for the SOFCs model are shown in Table 3. 

Eqs. (1) and (2) describe momentum transfer in the gas channel and porous electrodes, respectively, with Eq. (2) representing a modified Navier–Stokes equation incorporating Darcy's law. The mass transfer can be described by Eq. (3), which applies the Maxwell–Stefan model to capture multi-component gas diffusion. The effects of Knudsen diffusion within the porous electrodes are also included to improve accuracy [28,29]. The heat balance equation (Eq. (4)) is used to describe the heat transfer process within the SOFCs. The heat source term in this equation 

accounts for the contributions of Joule heating, chemical heat, and electrochemical heat [30]. _Qelec_ represents the electrochemical heat, where ΔS denotes the molar entropy change of the electrochemical reaction. _Qohm_ and _Qchem_ represent the heat of ohmic resistance and chemical reactions, respectively. Moreover, it should be noted that heat transfer occurs through conduction, convection, and radiation. In the present model, thermal radiation is neglected to reduce computational complexity. However, previous studies have shown that surface radiation can enhance heat dissipation and thereby reduce internal temperature differences within solid oxide cells [22]. Hence, with the consideration of thermal radiation, the temperature gradient could be lower. 

# _2.1.2. Chemical reaction_ 

Chemical reaction model is used to define the rates of chemical reactions occurring within SOFCs. Three main chemical reactions, including water-gas shift reaction (CO + H2O↔CO2 + H2, WGSR), methane carbon dioxide reforming reaction (CH4 + CO2↔2H2 + 2CO, DRM), and methane steam reforming reaction (CH4 + H2O↔3H2 + CO, MSR) are considered. Since MSR, DRM, and WGSR are all catalytic reactions, the Ni active sites within the anode serve as the primary locations for these reactions to proceed efficiently. More importantly, these reactions are difficult to occur in the catalyst-free flow channel at the actual operating temperature of SOFCs [35–37]. Therefore, we assume that the chemical reactions only occur in the anode [38,39].Table 4 summarizes the mathematical equations used in the chemical model.

- (3) An imbalance between heat generation and consumption can lead to significant temperature gradients within SWGG-SOFCs. DOE analysis shows that operating temperature is the dominant factor influencing the gradient, while the anode inlet flow rate has the weakest effect. Operating voltage exhibits a nonlinear influence. In addition, synergistic effects are observed between voltage and temperature, and between voltage and flow rate. 

The limitations of this study should also be pointed out. Carbon deposition was not considered, yet it plays an important role in longterm stability; incorporating this reaction will be essential for more accurate predictions in future work. In addition, the analysis was limited to steady-state, isothermal conditions and the single-cell level. Future studies should address the dynamic behavior and stack-level issues under realistic operating scenarios. 

Despite these limitations, the findings provide valuable theoretical insights and practical guidance for optimizing the operation of SWGGSOFCs.
