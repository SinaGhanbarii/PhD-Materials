### Introduction 

The water gas-shift (WGS) reaction has a large number of applications in the hydrogen production and the ammonia synthesis industry using coal, oil and natural gas as raw materials. It is also commonly used for carbon monoxide removal in hydrogen production via steam reforming and new technologies such as hydrogen fuel cells (HFCs) [1] and integrated gasification combined cycle (IGCC) [2e5]. 



Due to its exothermic nature, it is difficult to achieve a high conversion at a fast reaction rate under high temperature. As a result, the WGS reaction process is implemented in two steps in the industry: rapid reaction at high temperature (>350<sup>�</sup> C), and enhancement of reaction extent at low temperature (<300<sup>�</sup> C) [6]. According to the difference of WGS temperature, the catalysts can be roughly divided into two types: hightemperature shift catalysts and low-temperature shift catalysts. The former are mainly based on FeeCr and Ni, while the latter are mainly based on Cu [7,8]. In addition, some noble metal catalysts such as Pt have shown excellent catalytic performance compared with traditional catalysts, making it possible to be the next generation WGS reaction catalysts [9]. 

Compared with the traditional fixed-bed reactors, the catalytic membrane reactors can simultaneously perform WGS reaction and hydrogen separation, thereby obtaining higher reaction conversion and hydrogen purity. To date, there has been extensive research on membrane reactors, which is not limited to WGS reaction, but also includes various steam reforming reactions [3,10e12]. Hydrogen-selective membranes are the key to achieve high performance of membrane reactors, because removing hydrogen can change the composition of the reaction zone and thus break the chemical equilibrium in line with Le Chatelier's principle [13]. At present, there are many kinds of membranes such as Pd-based, MFI zeolite-based, silica and CMS membranes [14e17], among which Pd-based membranes are considered to be ideal membranes for hydrogen separation and purity due to their high selectivity and permeability, and have been widely studied in catalytic membrane reactors [7]. Tosti et al. [18] conducted a long-term test on the performance of the PdeAg alloy membrane over one year. The results showed that the membrane hydrogen permeability was stable around at 1:0� 10<sup>�8</sup> mol m<sup>�1</sup> s<sup>�1</sup> Pa<sup>�0:5</sup> measured at 350e400<sup>�</sup> C, and no defect or crack was found in the membrane. This implied that the Pdbased membrane not only had extremely high permeability, but also had good stability and durability. 

In the past two decades, a considerable amount of literature has reported the performance and feasibility of WGS reaction catalytic membrane reactors from different aspects. Chein et al. [19] established a two dimensional steady-state model of high temperature WGS reaction, mainly studying the influence of membrane permeability and steam to carbon ratio on the carbon monoxide conversion. Their results suggested that at low permeability the CO conversion was close to that of traditional reactors, while at high permeability the CO conversion was greatly improved. Based on the experimental results, Hla et al. [20] established a two dimensional non- 

isothermal model and used it to analyze the effect of catalyst content and temperature on the reactor performance. They found that increasing the amount of catalyst and reaction temperature could achieve high reactor performance, but also pointed out that excessive catalyst content and inlet temperature would induce an increase in temperature, leading to a deviation from the reasonable temperature. Adrover et al. [21] mainly discussed the CO conversion and thermal effect of WGS reaction varying with operating pressure. They claimed that the conversion increased obviously with increasing operating pressure and the temperature inside the reactor would also rise slightly. 

In fact, there are many other factors affecting membrane reactor performance including, space velocity, material diffusion, reaction kinetics and reactor geometry in addition to the factors mentioned above, and the interplay among them can-not be ignored. However, most published papers do not pay attention to the interaction between multiple factors, reactor configuration and operating conditions varying in different work, which makes some conclusions do not have universal applicability. Brunetti et al. [22,23] first proposed the volume index (VI) to analyze the advantage of membrane reactors over fixed-bed reactors, and then studied the effect of dimensionless parameter Damkohler number on reactor performance. Murmura et al. [24,25] applied Darcy's law to the gas flow through porous media in membrane reactors and established several crucial dimensionless parameters. By discussing the dimensionless parameters, the factors significantly affecting the performance of the membrane reactor under different operating conditions were determined. Besides, WGS reaction in catalytic membrane reactors is generally carried out under high temperature conditions, and less research is conducted under low temperatures. 

Herein, in this work a two-dimensional axisymmetric physical model of membrane reactor was established, based on low-temperature WGS reaction kinetics, and then the governing equations of the model were directly dimensionless to obtain dimensionless parameters. The aim here is to quantitatively analyze the effects of fluid flow rate, reaction rate, and permeate rate on the membrane reactor performance, providing some guidance for the design of membrane reactors. The main innovation of this paper is to use dimensionless parameters to analyze the membrane reactor system. And how the WGS reaction kinetics determine reaction behavior inside the reactor is discussed in detail. In addition, in order to ensure the accuracy of the model, the numerical results are compared with the experimental results.

### Conclusion 

The overall performance of the low-temperature water-gas reaction membrane reactor is studied by developing a CFD model based on the assumptions of isothermal reaction and laminar flow. To gain a better understanding of this issue, the reaction phenomenon inside the reactor is discussed in detail afterwards in terms of WGS reaction kinetics, Damkohler number (Da) and membrane permeability (Pp). We found that high operating temperatures are not only necessary to demonstrate the separation effect of the membrane from the perspective of thermodynamic equilibrium, but also can accelerate the reaction. At temperature of 573 K, the best Da and Pp values are found to be 10<sup>�4</sup> and 10<sup>�1</sup> respectively, corresponding to the optimal hydrogen recovery and CO conversion (nearly 100%). Then, we propose that taking the CO content and hydrogen content at the outlet of the reactor as two indicators of the degree of completion of the reaction and hydrogen separation, the optimal reactor design length can be well determined. Furthermore, the effect of sweep flow rates and sweep gas modes on the performance of the reactor is discussed. The results show that using sweep gas on the permeate side is necessary to improve the performance of the reactor with large hydrogen permeation flux. The most pronounced enhancement in reactor performance is observed for sweep gas flow rate of 0.2e0.5 L/min. And at this flow rate, further increase of reactor performance can be achieved by configuring the counter-current sweep gas mode. Finally, the research strategy in this article can also provide a reference for the research of different membrane reactors.
