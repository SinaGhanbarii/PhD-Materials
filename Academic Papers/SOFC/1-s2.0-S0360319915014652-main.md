Curve (b) of Fig. 3B presents the impedance diagram obtained in similar conditions but at 805<sup>�</sup> C. It shows the succession of a series resistance R1 and three R//CPE loops, with corresponding R and C values given in Table 3. The R1 value is smaller than that measured at 750<sup>�</sup> C, in accordance with usually reported variations of YSZ conductivities with temperature, following an Arrhenius law [36]. The two following loops appear nearly merged, and can be modeled either by one or two R//CPE circuits. The associated R2 or (R2A þ R2B) values are lower than those at 750<sup>�</sup> C, demonstrating that the associated phenomena are thermodynamically activated. On the contrary the R3/CPE3 loop is unchanged, with comparable R3 and C3 values, suggesting that this loop is associated to a phenomenon which is not dependent on temperature, such as diffusion [31,44e47]. The charge transfer phenomena, which are thermodynamically activated, are even less detectable than at 750<sup>�</sup> C [40,44,47]. 

Table 3 also collects the values obtained for the impedance diagram obtained �a 780<sup>�</sup> C, and confirms both the dependence of R1 and (R2A þ R2B) and the independence of R3 and C3 on temperature. 

The intersection of the low frequency part of the diagram with the real axis allows the determination of the total resistance of the cell. This resistance corresponds also to the slope of the curve U ¼ f(J) at the origin. Table 2 shows that the values of this total resistance when calculated from the impedance data (RpN) and estimated from U ¼ f(J) curve (RpU-J) are similar, e and comparable to values from literature [29 33]. Impedance measurements allow also to determine R1/Rp and (Rp-R1)/Rp values, corresponding respectively to the contribution of the electrolyte and of the electrodes to the total resistance Rp of the cell. When increasing temperature, the increase of the YSZ conductivity in less important that the thermal activation of the reaction taking place at the electrodes, resulting in a slight decrease of the contribution of the electrodes from 57 to 52%.

### Conclusion 

In this study, the potential use of Fiaxell YSZ-based solid oxide cell with different H2eCOeCO2eN2 mixtures has been demonstrated in the 750<sup>�</sup> Ce850<sup>�</sup> C range, and it has been shown that in these conditions the presence of carbon oxides CO2 and CO do not damage the cell. Indeed, CO is an electrochemical reactant as good as H2 or even better but only when the percentage of the fuel in the Fuel/N2 mixture is lower than 27%, which is the case for wood gasifier. Impedance measurements show that this limitation of acceptable amount of CO is due to mass transfer limitations. It has been shown that due to the water gas shift reaction, the composition 20% H2, 20% CO and 60% N2 leads to higher power densities than a 40% H2e60%N2 mixture and that the presence of CO2 induces no decrease of the cell performance. Results show that CO2 acts both in reducing the OCV voltage, and then the slope of the U/J curve and corresponding total cell resistance, but also as a reactant in the water gas shift reaction. Moreover, the effect of the presence of CH4, in quantities corresponding to the composition of the gasifier exhaust, has been also studied, and, due to the occurrence of the reforming reaction, induces no negative effect. All these conclusions demonstrate that the Fiaxell cells can be used successfully with wet H2eCOeCO2eCH4eN2 corresponding to the exhaust of the fixed bed downdraft S3D gasifier. Additional experiments are still in progress in order to study more precisely the aging of the cells under these H2eCOeCO2eCH4e N2 mixtures, and the effect of small amounts of H2S and HCl, which are also present in the exhaust of the gazifier. Simultaneously, a general effort is also in progress in order to improve the performance of the cells by optimizing the design of the cell in order to reduce resistances due to CO mass transfer reaction.
