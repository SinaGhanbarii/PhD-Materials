In this work, an improved membrane characterization, computational fluid dynamic (CFD) simulations of inert particle beds, and simulations of a membrane reactor for Water Gas Shift (WGS) are coupled together to evaluate the concentration polarization distribution along Pd-based membrane reactors. To perform this investigation, first a 3.6 mm-thick Pd75Ag25-membrane is characterized using experimental data from the literature as input to an improved approach allowing the non-ideality of internal diffusion to be quantitatively evaluated as a function of hydrogen partial pressure. Then, CFD simulations of a particle bed composed of regular arrays of mono-disperse spherical particles are carried out, showing that the velocity field between particles and membranes contributes to enhance the mass transfer towards the membrane surface. Finally, after validating a 1D-1D reactor model by literature experimental data in terms of both conversion and recovery index vs. feed pressure, simulations of concentration polarization coefficient (CPC) profiles along the reactor are performed. These profiles allow a clearly identification of the reactor zones where polarization is higher, providing useful information to minimize the polarization influence on reactor performances. ## Introduction 

Membrane-assisted reactors offer a unique opportunity to combine reaction and separation in a single device, reducing the costs in terms of plant volume, better raw materials 

exploitation and energy efficiency [1]. In designing a membrane device, one of the most important aspects is the fluid dynamic condition, which is the key-issue to act on to reduce the mass transfer resistance outside membrane, which is named as “concentration polarization” on the feed side.

Concentration polarization is an important and often underestimated phenomenon affecting the membrane processes. For this reason, an approach able to systematically take into account this phenomenon is needed for an efficient design of membrane-assisted devices. 

To reduce the influence of such a phenomenon, due to the resistance of the less permeating species to the flux of the more permeating ones [2e18], several design solutions were proposed, such as micro-structured membrane modules [19], fluidized bed reactors/permeators [20] and devices based on turbulence-promoted flow [5e10,21e24]. 

In general, the effect of concentration polarization is gradually stronger for increasing membrane selectivity and permeance. Therefore, it is quite important, for example, in hydrogen permeation through Pd-based membranes, which show an infinite selectivity with respect to the other species by letting only hydrogen pass through. 

Concentration polarization quantifying is not an easy issue, as it is usually difficult to measure the external mass transfer resistance directly from experimental data. More often, simulation of the considered membrane device is performed by using an appropriate transport model matching the measurable device performance, basically conversion and recovery. This is because the actual concentration of the species in the neighbourhood of membrane surface is generally unknown, especially in systems where the flow is interrupted by obstacles (like catalyst particles or tube bundles) enhancing the external mass transfer. 

A possible approach to simulation of these types of systems could be to use techniques of computational fluid dynamics (CFD), implementing the NaviereStokes equations along with the appropriate constitutive equations and boundary conditions with a brute-force approach to reproduce at best the real fluid dynamics behaviour of the flow in the device taking into account the heterogeneity of the system (i.e., the presence of catalyst particles and obstacles). If successful, such a simulation would provide us with all information needed to evaluate the external mass transfer resistance in each axial position of membrane surface. 

To do that, however, there are several issues to deal with. In fact, even in fully-developed laminar conditions, obstacles can induce local turbulences generating vortices, whose presence is not easy to take into account by CFD. This could make the actual fluid dynamic behaviour quite different from the simulated one, this resulting in over or under estimation of the external mass transfer resistance and, thus, of the overall device performance. To partially address this issue, appropriate turbulence models could be used locally [25e30]. 

However, the necessary computational time could make the simulation prohibitive. Therefore, in most cases simplified constitutive mass and heat transfer models based on semiempirical but well-established correlations are used within mono-dimensional conservation equations. 

In this context, the present work uses a systematic approach to evaluate the concentration polarization coefficient (CPC) profiles in catalytic-bed membrane reactors. For this purpose, a high-performance Pd-based membrane of literature [31] is characterized by an improved approach to evaluate the intrinsic permeation parameters in terms of permeability and non-ideal contribution of internal diffusion. 

Then, the so-evaluated membrane properties are used to perform computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations considering a particle bed with two different values of particle size. 

As well, the same membrane properties are input into a 1D-1D transport reactor model to simulate the behaviour of a membrane reactor for Water-Gas Shift, including the radial hydrogen concentration drop owing to the presence of the non-permeating species involved in the reaction. The model is validated with experimental reaction data from the literature in terms of both CO conversion and H2 recovery [31]. 

## Modelling and simulation approach 

### Model of membrane reactors 

Based on the approach adopted in this work, simulations of concentration polarization in particle-bed membrane reactors is the result of coupling between a permeation model of the hydrogen transport through membrane and a model of reactor. 

As for the permeation model, Fig. 1 shows the two steps considered: 1) the external mass transfer from the bulk of the particles bed towards the membrane surface and 2) the internal diffusion of hydrogen through the Pd-based membrane selective (dense) layer. Since in the permeate side pure hydrogen is considered, no transport equations are needed (flat radial pressure profile). 

As for the first step, an already developed and validated multicomponent-based permeation model is improved [2,32,33] to account for the presence of the particle (catalyst) bed. Such improvements consist in using both an effective diffusivity corrected by the εBed/tBed ratio (¼0.51/1.7) and the effective inter-particle velocity to estimate the mass transfer film thickness [34]. Concerning the diffusion in the Pd-layer, it is modelled by the irreversible thermodynamics theory, for which the hydrogen chemical potential gradient is the driving force, considering the non-ideality of diffusion coefficient. ### CFD simulation for concentration polarization 

In parallel with using mono-dimensional models, the influence of concentration polarization is preliminary investigated here also by computational fluid dynamics (CFD) techniques applied to beds of particles. For this purpose, the concentration polarization behaviour is analyzed in an inert 20 cm-long tubular particle-bed (ID ¼ 2.54 cm) equipped with a single 5 mm-thick supported membrane at 360<sup>�</sup> C. 

Such an investigation is carried out to give a preliminary idea of the polarization level in beds of particles, whose fluid dynamics is similar to that in catalytic chemical reactors. 

For this purpose, the NaviereStokes equations coupled with the mass conservation ones are implemented in Comsol Multiphysics<sup>®</sup> considering a H2/N2 binary mixture with a hydrogen feed composition of 98.6% flowing along a particlebed of regularly-distributed spherical particles. The use of such a high value of hydrogen composition is made to consider a wider range of compositions, starting from a maximum composition close to the unity e corresponding to values typical of high-purification processes (where concentration polarization level close to zero) e for then decreasing it down to lower values typical of more standard separation processes. 



Fig. 2 e Configuration considered in the reactor simulation. Five tubular membranes equally distributed over the crosssectional area of reactor. 

For simulation, a 2-D axial-symmetry geometry is setup (see Fig. 6a in Section “Evaluation of CPC profiles by CFD Simulation”), taking into account the heterogeneity of the bed by drawing the regular array of circles, which are considered dense and impermeable to gas (i.e., no slip condition at the particle wall). As a 2-D axial symmetry is used, the circles in 2-D becomes toroidal forms in 3-D. Therefore, the considered structure is actually composed of an array of rings, around which the gas mixture flows. In doing that, what is neglected in this system with respect to a real3-D one is the part of the flow around particles owing to the presence of the toroidal wall, which makes the cross-sectional area available to flow smaller than that offered by spherical particles. 

Concerning the geometrical properties of the system, the considered membrane length is 20 cm with an outer diameter of 1 mm, whereas two different values of particle diameter are used to pack the shell (dPart ¼ 3.10 mm and 1.53 mm, respectively). In particular, in the construction of the considered 2D particle array, contact with particles is prevented, in order to avoid the retentate channel to be closed to the gas flow. In this way we want to analyze the effect of the perturbation induced by the presence of obstacles (particles) with respect to the empty channel case in a condition of low packing degree. 

The values of the voidage degree (εBed) e calculated to be 0.85 for the smaller-particle bed and 0.8 for the larger-particle one e are evaluated based on the 2D geometry, i.e., by the ratio of the void surface and the total one. Therefore, its value is actually different from the 3D one, remarking that these geometrical conditions, which are actually closer to fluidised beds ones, are considered just to describe practically the use of the EAC coefficient in determining the polarization extent from simulation data. The used boundary conditions for momentum transfer are: velocity field set at the inlet, total pressure set at the outlet and no-slip condition set on the membrane and particles surfaces. 

As for the mass transfer equations, the used boundary conditions are: compositions set at the inlet, convective flux set at the outlet, conditions of impermeability on particle surface for all species (H2 and N2) and on membrane surface for N2 only. The required hydrogen flux that has to be set on membrane surface is calculated by using the only internal diffusion step of the previously mentioned hydrogen permeation model, as the external mass transfer on the particle bed side is solved by the CFD solver. 

As for CPC, its value at a certain reactor position is evaluated by first defining an appropriate characteristic permeation driving force (the Sieverts one) and then evaluating such a driving force both between the bulks of the gas phases and between the membrane surfaces on feed and permeate side. Concerning this point, we choose to take the hydrogen partial pressure of bulk as the maximum value along the radial direction at each position of reactor. 

In fact, in general, owing to the presence of particles, the velocity and pressure field can be sufficiently complicated that it could be generally impossible to recognize a bulk value, as the definition of bulk would be questionable as well. Therefore, in this particular case, the bulk value of hydrogen partial pressure used to calculate CPC at a certain axial position z is evaluated by considering the maximum value of partial pressure at that position, whereas the membrane value is directly evaluated at the membrane surface. It is noticed that such a choice gives the highest value of the CPC. 

Let us remark that the more standard characterization using the Sieverts law (Eq. (3)) does not take into account the permeability variation with hydrogen concentration, as a linear regression is used independently of the trend of the experimental data. 



In this sense, the regression results can be referred to as a sort of average with respect to the hydrogen concentration, considering the distance between data and fitting straight line a discrepancy due to just experimental error. 

However, for increasing hydrogen concentration in the feed and/or permeate side, the discrepancy from the straight line is more and more caused by the effect of the non-ideal diffusion [35,41], whose behaviour implies a more complete membrane characterization. 

As described elsewhere, the improved approach used in this work makes use of a third-order polynomial form without the second-order term to fit the experimental flux vs. the square root of the feed pressure at fixed permeate pressure [35,36,41]. As a result, the hydrogen permeability can be evaluated as a function of the hydrogen concentration (or, equivalently, pressure). As mentioned above, the experimental data reported in Brunetti et al. (2015) [31] are used to perform this analysis. 

Fig. 3 shows this type of results at different temperatures. First of all, let us remind that this permeability is referred to as “intrinsic” because, for hydrogen pressure approaching to zero, the effect of internal stresses caused by the hydrogen permeation in the metal lattice is negligible and independent of hydrogen concentration. The values of the intrinsic permeability can be read in this type of plots at a hydrogen pressure approaching to zero, corresponding to a state of infinite dilution of the hydrogen atoms in the metal lattice. 

Based on this approach, the explicit expression reported in Eq. (2) is used for CPC calculation. 



This definition implies that CPC approaches zero for negligible polarization whereas approaches the unity for maximum one [2].

### Simulation of membrane reactor for WGS 

Before simulating WGS in a Pd-based membrane reactor, the reactor model including concentration polarization is validated using literature data [31] in terms of CO conversion (Fig. 8a) and H2 Recovery Index (Fig. 8b) vs. total feed pressure. The observed good agreement between experimental and simulated data, indicates the model efficacy to reproduce the actual reactor behaviour. 

Such a good agreement is established without any adjustable parameter. The only arbitrary choice for parameters is the catalyst intra-particle tortuosity, supposed to vary within a range of 2e7. However, the simulation sensitivity to this geometrical property is observed to be low and, thus, a tortuosity value of 3 is used in all the subsequent calculation. 

Furthermore, it is found that the conversion is slightly increasing with total pressure (actually almost constant), whereas the recovery index increases significantly with it. This indicates that, under the conditions considered in the reaction tests, the kinetics is weakly influenced by the presence of the membrane, which basically acts to increase the pure hydrogen recovery extent in the permeate. In fact, the WGS kinetics is thermodynamically not influenced by the total pressure and the positive effect of the pressure is due to the membrane, which allows a continuous hydrogen removal pushing the equilibrium towards the products. 

Therefore, the validation of the used concentration polarization model is more effective in this case if made in terms of hydrogen recovery, which is much more sensitive to the polarization influence. ## Conclusions 

In this work, the concentration polarization distribution in Pdbased membrane reactors for Water Gas shift was investigated by a simulation approach coupling an improved characterization of a 3.6 mm-thick membrane using permeation data from the literature, CFD simulations of particle beds and a complex model of membrane reactor. 

First, a real 3.6 mm-thick membrane by an improved approach allowing the determination of both hydrogen permeability and non-ideality index of hydrogen internal diffusion as a function of temperature and hydrogen pressure. 

Then, the flow of a binary mixture of H2/N2 through a particle bed of a regular 2D arrays of mono-disperse spherical particles was analyzed by computational fluid dynamics simulations. This analysis showed that the velocity field between particles and membranes contributes to enhance the mass transfer towards the membrane surface and that the particle size does not provide an appreciable contribution in
